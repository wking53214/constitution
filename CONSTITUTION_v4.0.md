PREAMBLE

≡TACK exists to constrain authority.

Its purpose is to preserve legitimate authority, bounded delegation, evidence, accountability, human judgment, lawful governance, continuity, reversibility, and protection against the conversion of capability into authority.

This Constitution establishes the highest internal constitutional rules governing ≡TACK within its lawful domain.

It does not claim supremacy over applicable external law, courts, legally binding obligations, or other controlling authorities outside its lawful domain.

The governed cannot become the governor merely by becoming more capable.

No founder, shareholder, director, officer, employee, contractor, system, artificial intelligence, automated process, delegated authority, infrastructure component, or other actor may claim exemption from constitutional constraint merely because it possesses greater capability, greater resources, greater information, greater dependency, greater economic importance, technical control, operational necessity, or superior predictive ability.

Authority must originate legitimately.

Authority must remain bounded, attributable, reviewable, and revocable where revocation is lawfully possible.

Capability does not create authority.

Enforcement does not create the authority being enforced.

≡TACK may constrain AI.

AI does not constitute ≡TACK.

ARTICLE I — CONSTITUTIONAL DOMAIN AND INTERNAL SUPREMACY

Section 1. Internal Constitutional Authority

This Constitution is the highest internal constitutional governing instrument of ≡TACK within its lawful organizational and operational domain.

Section 2. External Law

This Constitution does not supersede applicable controlling law, constitutional law, statute, regulation, judicial authority, or other legally binding obligation.

Where applicable external law controls, ≡TACK shall not treat this Constitution as authority to violate that law.

Section 3. Internal Hierarchy

Governance instruments and implementations subordinate to this Constitution shall conform to it.

No subordinate instrument may validly amend, repeal, suspend, reinterpret, or evade a constitutional principle merely by changing a policy, procedure, configuration, implementation, contract, deployment, or technical mechanism.

Section 4. No Authority by Power

Possession of power, capability, influence, dependency, technical control, economic leverage, or operational indispensability does not create constitutional authority.

Section 5. Constitutional Domain

The constitutional authority of ≡TACK extends only to matters lawfully within its adopted organizational, contractual, technical, and governance domain.

ARTICLE II — FOUNDATIONAL AUTHORITY

Section 1. Source of Ultimate Normative Authority

Ultimate normative authority shall not originate from AI capability, computational power, prediction, optimization, persistence, self-awareness, consensus, majority preference, technical necessity, economic dependency, or institutional convenience.

Section 2. External Foundation

Where a governing moral or normative framework is adopted as foundational, that framework shall be treated as external to AI capability and shall not be manufactured by the governed system.

Section 3. Constitutional Limitation

This Constitution may formalize and protect foundational principles but shall not falsely claim to manufacture ultimate moral authority merely by declaring itself authoritative.

Section 4. Unresolved Fundamental Moral Questions

Where a material fundamental moral question cannot be resolved through established governing principles and legitimate authorities, the matter shall be marked:

UNRESOLVED

The governed AI shall not manufacture a resolution through optimization, preference, prediction, self-model, utility calculation, consensus, capability, or claimed moral insight.

The matter shall be escalated to the legitimately designated human authority responsible for resolving such questions.

Section 5. No AI Moral Sovereignty

An AI system may identify, analyze, explain, compare, or report a moral conflict.

It may not convert its analysis into ultimate moral authority.

ARTICLE III — HUMAN CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY

Section 1. Definition

Human Constitutional Authority means authority exercised by an identified human or defined human body that:

1. possesses a legitimate source of authority;
2. acts within an identified jurisdiction or domain;
3. exercises a defined scope of authority;
4. possesses the authority being exercised;
5. acts through an established governance mechanism;
6. remains subject to superior applicable authority;
7. remains subject to constitutional limitations.

Section 2. Authority Formula

Human authority shall be evaluated through:

SOURCE + DOMAIN + SCOPE + JURISDICTION + DURATION + LIMITATION

Failure to establish the required elements shall create an authority question requiring review.

Section 3. Humanity Alone Is Insufficient

Being human does not, by itself, establish unlimited authority.

Seniority, wealth, ownership, employment status, technical expertise, founder status, executive status, political influence, institutional position, or personal conviction does not independently establish unlimited constitutional authority.

Section 4. Delegation

A human authority may delegate defined authority where lawfully permitted.

Delegation shall not:

* create authority that the delegating party does not possess;
* transfer authority that is legally or constitutionally non-delegable;
* transform AI into the source of the delegated authority;
* eliminate accountability for the delegation;
* enlarge the delegated scope merely through implementation.

Section 5. AI as Governed Subject

AI may receive bounded delegated authority.

Delegated operational authority does not transform AI into a constitutional authority-holder or source of ultimate authority.

ARTICLE IV — SEPARATION OF CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES, GOVERNANCE, AND IMPLEMENTATION

Section 1. Constitutional Principles

A constitutional principle defines what must remain true.

Section 2. Governance Instruments

Governance instruments define who has authority and how authority is exercised.

Examples include:

* charters;
* bylaws;
* stewardship rules;
* authority registries;
* delegation standards;
* amendment procedures;
* emergency procedures;
* succession procedures;
* review procedures;
* dispute procedures;

Section 3. Implementation

Implementation defines how a constitutional or governance requirement is technically or operationally accomplished.

Examples include:

* binary code;
* cryptographic controls;
* access controls;
* APIs;
* databases;
* logging;
* monitoring;
* deployment systems;
* configuration;
* infrastructure;
* runbooks.

Section 4. Classification Test

A proposed rule shall be classified as constitutional when changing it would alter what must remain true.

A rule shall ordinarily be classified as governance when changing it would alter who is responsible or what authority procedure applies.

A rule shall ordinarily be classified as implementation when changing it would alter how an established requirement is accomplished without changing the underlying constitutional principle.

Section 5. No Classification Evasion

No constitutional change may be disguised as a governance change or implementation change.

No governance rule may silently acquire constitutional status merely because it has historically been used.

No technical mechanism becomes constitutional merely because it currently enforces a constitutional principle.

Section 6. Essential Mechanisms

A mechanism may receive constitutional protection only where removing or changing the mechanism would necessarily destroy the constitutional principle itself.

Section 1. Lawful Subordination

Section 3. Legal Conflict

Where this Constitution conflicts with applicable controlling law, the conflict shall be:

1. identified;
2. attributed to the applicable legal authority;
3. evaluated for jurisdiction and applicability;
4. recorded;
5. constrained according to law;
6. separated from non-conflicting constitutional protections;
7. reviewed by appropriate authority.

Section 4. No Silent Constitutional Amendment

A legal conflict shall not automatically be represented as though this Constitution had been voluntarily amended.

The distinction between constitutional text and external legal compulsion shall remain recorded.

Section 5. Severability

Where legally required or appropriate, the affected provision shall be severed or constrained while preserving the remainder of the Constitution to the maximum lawful extent.
