## Role Within ≡TACK

- **What it is.** The written statement of the rules ≡TACK is meant to be held to: authority must originate legitimately, stay bounded and revocable, and capability does not create authority. It also says enforcement does not create the authority being enforced.
- **What it owns.** The wording of those principles and of the articles below. It is the one place the principles are written down.
- **What the document claims for itself.** Article I declares it the highest internal governing instrument of ≡TACK within its lawful domain, and says the governance instruments and implementations under it are to conform to it. That is a statement in the text. This repository holds no record that the document was adopted, and nothing here checks conformance (INSPECTED 2026-11-18: the repository is one file in one commit).
- **What it does not own.** Any code, test, enforcement or evidence. Nothing in this repository checks that another repository follows it. It does not claim supremacy over external law (its own Article I and V set that limit).
- **Depends on / depended on by.** It imports nothing. Whether any repository cites or checks it: UNKNOWN (not inspected in this pass). The CNS repository is the one that holds machine-checked contracts; this document is not one of them.
- **Verified versus declared.** Everything here is declared. No claim in this document has been verified by running anything.
- **Status.** A candidate. Its own status line says "Developed Candidate". Article XV says an amendment needs a formally established amendment process and, with a narrow exception, that no single actor may amend the text alone. It also says a change in practice, interpretation or implementation cannot quietly amend the text. No amendment process is written down in this repository (INSPECTED 2026-11-18). Whether one exists elsewhere is UNKNOWN.

## What is in the document

62 numbered articles (counted 2026-11-15), in this order of subject:

- Domain, internal supremacy and its limits, and where authority comes from: Articles I to III.
- Separation of principles, governance and implementation, and the limit of external law: Articles IV and V.
- Truth, evidence, delegation and separation of governance functions, due process: Articles VI to X.
- AI authority, governance infrastructure, security, emergencies: Articles XI to XIV.
- Amendment, anti-capture, no self-escape, reversibility, continuity, dissolution, interpretation, review, minimality: Articles XV to XXV.
- Rules for AI systems about themselves (self-model, self-modification, goals, self-preservation, replication, deception, instrumental power, consciousness claims, internal state, recursion, collective action, influence): Articles XXVI to XXXVIII.
- Later articles on resource control, locks, unknown and future capability, conflicting authority, authority by persistence, escalation or jurisdictional escape, no benevolent exception, interoperability, exit, graceful degradation and final invariants: Articles XXXIX to LXII.

Read the document itself for exact wording. This README summarizes subjects only. It quotes two labels from the document's header (the status and the classification) and nothing from the articles. Besides the 62 articles, the document has a preamble and a closing section, which are not summarized here.

## What this is not

- It is not software, and it is not a verifier. Having a constitution does not mean any system complies with it.
- It is not legal advice or a legally binding instrument by itself.
- Article IV keeps the principles separate from governance and from implementation. A mechanism that enforces a rule does not become constitutional because of that.
